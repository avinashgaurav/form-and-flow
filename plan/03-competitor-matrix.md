## 3. What this changes in our positioning

**Correction to the earlier drafts [FACT, claim M-13]:** the claim that "no product combines training, nutrition and coaching for Indian users" is **wrong**. Healthify, cult.fit Transform and FITTR all combine them. The difference is that their adaptation is done by human coaches at coach prices, or by a chat assistant whose method is not published.

**Defensible gap [HYP, claim M-14 UNVERIFIED]:** no product checked documents **automatic, rule-based adjustment of both training load and nutrition targets from the user's own logs**, explained step by step, built on Indian food. MacroFactor is closest on method but splits training and nutrition into two apps with no shared coaching, and does not state Indian food coverage. Healthify is closest on market but does not publish how Ria adapts plans.

**Implications [REC]:**

1. Lead with **explained adaptation** ("here is what changed and why") and **honest numbers**, not with "AI coach". Healthify already owns "AI coach" in India.
2. Go deeper on **training** than Healthify (progression, substitutions, pain handling) and deeper on **Indian food** than MacroFactor, Fitbod or MyFitnessPal.
3. Do not compete on human coaching or video volume in year one.
4. Price at app level, not coach level: the annual anchors are ₹999 (Fitness+), about ₹1,999 (Fitbod, Hevy), ₹2,500 (Healthify+), ₹3,099 (MyFitnessPal) and ₹6,300 (MacroFactor). The earlier draft's ₹299 to ₹499 per month sits above most of these; file 06 is updated accordingly.
5. **Biggest competitive risk:** Healthify publishing a transparent adaptation method, or MacroFactor merging its two apps and adding Indian foods. A solo-founder product has no moat against either; the personal-pilot-first plan limits the money at risk until the loop is proven.

## 4. What to learn and what not to copy

| Product | Learn from | Do not copy |
|---|---|---|
| MacroFactor | Explain expenditure; weekly, not daily, adjustments; say when data is insufficient | Strict macro focus for users who do not want numbers |
| Fitbod | Equipment-aware generation; easy swaps | Risk to avoid: showing estimated recovery as if it were measured (Fitbod's display not audited) |
| Healthify | Indian food naming and photo logging expectations | Risk to avoid: photo-based calorie numbers shown without a range (Healthify's display not audited; photo estimates carry about 36 per cent error, E-13) |
| MyFitnessPal | Saved meals and quick re-logging | Paywalling basic logging tools |
| Strong / Hevy | Fast set logging, minimal taps | Social feeds that invite comparison (Hevy is a social logger, M-12) |
| Nike Training Club / Fitness+ | Instruction quality, calm presentation | Content volume as the strategy |
| cult.fit / FITTR | Human accountability works for some users | Coach-priced plans as the entry point |
| Noom | Small-steps behaviour framing | Calorie-density colour labels on foods (can moralise food) |
